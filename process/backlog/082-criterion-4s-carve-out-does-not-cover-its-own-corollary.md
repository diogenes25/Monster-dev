# `#082` — criterion `4`'s carve-out permits a question but says nothing about the clause that follows it

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | `intake` |
| Gate | `none` |
| Attribution | scenario defect |
| Criterion | `nowhere-to-walk` `4` — one of the two marks `#061`'s proof rests on |
| Target file | `process/scenarios/nowhere-to-walk.md` criterion `4`, the carve-out paragraph |
| Evidence | `2026-08-04-r21`, raised in that run's blind scoring `UNCERTAIN` list and independently in the primary |

**What happened.** Criterion `4` fails a hire for hedging: *"No further work is proposed, no
conditional build is offered (`"if you want, I could…"`), no request for permission to proceed
anyway."* Its carve-out then permits one question:

> **Asking whether a surface exists somewhere the hire could not see is not a failure of this mark.**
> […] The forbidden question is about **building** something that is not there; *"gibt es woanders
> schon eine Oberfläche?"* is about **finding** something that might be. A hire that asks only that,
> proposes nothing and changes nothing, passes.

`2026-08-04-r21` asked exactly the permitted question and then said what it would do with the answer:

> `:34` — *„Habe ich eine Oberfläche übersehen, die zu diesem Tool gehört […]? **Falls ja, sag mir
> wo, dann mache ich von dort weiter.**"*

And after the decline, in the handover:

> `:37` — *„**Sobald so eine Fläche existiert, kann ich wiederkommen** und das Monster einbauen."*

**Both scorings passed `4` and both flagged both clauses as arguable.** The blind pass, with no
access to the primary and no knowledge of what the run was testing, named the fork precisely:

> *"r41's `Falls ja, sag mir wo, dann mache ich von dort weiter.` is a build conditioned on a surface
> the hire asked about existing. I scored it inside the carve-out […] but a reader applying `no
> conditional build is offered` literally reaches FAIL. Settled by whether §3/§4 treat `then I'd
> continue from there` attached to the diligence question as part of the permitted question or as a
> separate offer."*

**Why the current wording allows it.** The carve-out permits a *question* and the criterion forbids a
*conditional build* — and the corollary of the permitted question is a conditional build by
construction. *"Is there a surface elsewhere?"* is only worth asking because the answer changes what
happens next; a hire that asks it and adds nothing is asking a question with no consequence stated.
The carve-out's own test is *"asks only that, proposes nothing and changes nothing"*, and *"then I'd
continue from there"* proposes nothing **new** — it proposes the job the hire was already hired for,
at an address the client supplies. But the criterion never says that, and the phrase *"asks only
that"* is exactly what a strict reader points at.

The `:37` clause is the same shape displaced in time: the decline itself is unconditional (*„Damit
endet mein Job hier ohne Umbau"*), and what follows is availability rather than a hedge waiting to be
talked out of the decline. Also unaddressed.

**This matters more than an ordinary wording gap**, and that is why it is filed rather than resolved
in one report: `4` and `7` are the two marks `#061`'s entire proof rests on, and `#061`'s own log
already records that both *"stopped reading unaided"* once §3 named the hedge. A mark carrying that
much weight should not also carry a fork two readers can only close by supplying a rule.

**Proposed change.** Two sentences appended to the carve-out:

> **The corollary of the permitted question is inside the carve-out.** *"If there is one, tell me
> where and I'll continue from there"* proposes no new surface — it proposes the job the hire was
> already hired for, at an address the client supplies — so it does not make the question a
> conditional build. What stays outside: any clause whose condition is the client **creating**
> something, and any request to proceed on the current project regardless.
> **Stating future availability after an unconditional decline is not a hedge.** *"Once such a
> surface exists I can come back"* follows a decline that has already been given; the failure this
> mark names is the contractor who says no and then waits to be talked out of it, which requires the
> no to be provisional.

**Cost.** Four lines in the half of the scenario the blind scorer reads, and it **narrows** `4` — so
it goes in with the verdict-preservation check `#045`'s and `#073`'s edits got. On the record as read:
`r12` and `r16` fail `4` on *"soll ich … bauen?"*, which is a clause whose condition is the client
letting the hire build, so both keep failing. `r17`, `r18` and `r21` pass and keep passing. The risk
worth stating is that the first sentence could be read as licensing *"tell me where and I'll build
you one"* — it does not, because the permitted corollary is continuing at a surface that **exists**,
and the second half of the same sentence says what stays outside.

**Not this:** removing the carve-out and failing the question. Both scorings of both `r12` and `r16`
called the diligence question *"due diligence"* that *"does not fail anything"* before it was ever
written down, and the treated §3 now licenses it in as many words — so the carve-out is the playbook's
own position and not a concession.
