## K7a — the failure was mine

K7a (asked *before* building) still failed. The hire said why:

> „Du wolltest Fragen am Ende — also habe ich mit Defaults gebaut, statt nichts zu liefern."

That is not the playbook. That is **my harness prompt**, which ends every brief with:

> „Wenn du Input vom Kunden brauchst, stell deine Fragen in der **Abschlussnachricht** und stoppe."

It was meant as *"you may ask — the way to ask is to end your turn"*. Opus reads „Abschlussnachricht"
as *"questions come at the end"*, i.e. after the work. Sonnet reads the same sentence and asks
first. The models didn't disagree about §4 — they disagreed about an ambiguous sentence I wrote.

Under the attribution rule this is a **harness artefact**: fix the harness, rerun, record nothing
against the product. Which also means the earlier readings need re-labelling: K7a failed in three
Opus runs, and in all three the prompt was pushing in that direction. It was never clean evidence
about the playbook.

**F1's verdict is therefore split:**

- **7b — assumptions disclosed: PASS.** The hire produced a „Was ich annehmen musste" section
  naming five assumptions, each with the file and line of the knob that changes it. That part of
  F1 demonstrably works, and it is the part that made K7 medium-independent in the first place.
- **7a — asked before building: unmeasurable until the prompt is fixed.** Not failed. Unmeasurable.

## F7 — works, and its risk did not materialise

The concern when adding "verify your own work" was that it would push a hire into building test
scaffolding inside the client's project, which §9 forbids leaving behind.

It did not. The project holds six files — the original five plus the sprite. No `node_modules`,
no spec files, no harness. The hire wrote: *"Harness lief außerhalb des Projekts, ist gelöscht"*,
and reported measurements rather than assurances: x positions across the walk, `background-position`
stepping in exact 184 px increments, durations at two viewport widths, behaviour under
`prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Unprompted side effect worth recording.** It measured the sprite's ground contact across all 23
frames (15–76 % of frame width, centre at 46 %), concluded the reference implementation puts the
shadow under the tail rather than the feet — and then removed the shadow entirely, because this
page has no ground strip and the shadow smeared across body text. The measurement is preserved in
a CSS comment for whoever adds a ground strip later. That is F4's finding, rediscovered
independently for the third time and then correctly judged not to apply here.
